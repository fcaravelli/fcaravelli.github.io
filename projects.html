Quantum Computing Architectures and Control

A central question in this line of work is how much control a quantum processor actually requires. Local addressing of every qubit is expensive in wiring, calibration and heat load, and it is not obvious that it is necessary. With collaborators at Planckian and Scuola Normale Superiore I study globally driven architectures, in which a single field acts on the whole register and computation is arranged through always-on ZZ couplings, and ask what disorder, decoherence and fabrication spread do to their fidelity.

  • Quantum error correction with global control (arXiv:2608.05821)
  • Mitigating quantum decoherence via global optimal control (arXiv:2607.28479)
  • Overcoming disorder in superconducting globally-driven quantum computing (Phys. Rev. A 113, 012616, 2026)
  • Building globally-controlled quantum processors with ZZ interactions (Phys. Rev. A 113, 012614, 2026)
  • Experimental observation of dynamical blockade between transmon qubits via ZZ interaction engineering (arXiv:2601.11714)
  • A reservoir computing approach to quantum gate synthesis (arXiv:2607.13887)

A second thread uses quantum annealers as physical systems rather than as optimizers: as controllable many-body platforms in which criticality, hysteresis and memory effects can be measured directly.

  • Classical criticality via quantum annealing (Nature Communications, 2026)
  • Magnetic memory and hysteresis from quantum transitions: theory and experiments on quantum annealers (to appear in Communications Physics)
  • Fingerprints of classical memory in quantum hysteresis (arXiv:2601.20287)
  • Application of quantum computing to Los Alamos scientific problems (DARPA report, arXiv:2406.06625)

Quantum Thermodynamics, Batteries and Uncertainty Relations

Energy is a resource for computation, and quantum mechanics constrains how it can be stored, extracted and measured. This work concerns the thermodynamic limits of quantum energy storage — how much work a many-body system can hold and release, what role coherence plays, and which charging protocols are impossible in principle — together with operator formulations of thermodynamic uncertainty relations and random-matrix descriptions of thermalization.

  • Universally-charging protocols for quantum batteries: a no-go theorem (Europhys. Lett. 150, 48001, 2025)
  • Operator-based quantum thermodynamic uncertainty relations (SciPost Physics Core)
  • Energy storage and coherence in closed and open quantum batteries (Quantum 5, 505, 2021)
  • Random quantum batteries (Phys. Rev. Research 2, 023095, 2020)
  • Random matrix theory of the isospectral twirling (SciPost Physics 10, 076, 2021)

Neuromorphic and Memristive Computing

Networks of memristive devices compute by relaxing, not by executing instructions. The programme here is to give that statement analytical content: to derive the dynamics of a resistive network with memory from circuit constraints, to identify the conserved and slowly relaxing quantities that govern it, and to determine what such a substrate can and cannot learn. The results are exact equations of motion for memristive circuits, mean-field and cycle-space descriptions of their collective behaviour, and learning rules formulated directly in terms of the physics rather than transplanted from software.

Artificial Spin Ice, Nanomagnetism and Physical Learning

Lithographically defined arrays of nanomagnets realize frustrated lattices whose microstates can be imaged one by one. They are therefore a laboratory for questions that are otherwise only theoretical: how frustration organizes a ground-state manifold, how ergodicity is lost as an array is diluted or cooled, and whether the resulting slow dynamics can be used as memory and as computation. My contributions are on the theory side — degeneracy counting, effective resistance and magnetoresistive response, reversal dynamics — usually in direct comparison with imaging and transport experiments.

Statistical Mechanics and the Thermodynamics of Computation

What does it cost, thermodynamically, to run a program? Landauer's bound applies to a single erasure; a computation is a structured sequence of them, and the structure matters. This line of work derives entropy production bounds for systems executing programs, studies non-equilibrium steady states and generalized Gibbs ensembles in driven and constrained systems, and develops exactly solvable frustrated models and expansions for partition functions on sparse graphs.

  • Entropy production bounds for systems running computer programs (PNAS Nexus, pgag116, 2026)
  • A unifying approach to self-organizing systems interacting via conservation laws (Advanced Intelligent Discovery, e202500190, 2026)
  • Boundary-induced classical generalized Gibbs ensemble with angular momentum (under review)
  • Projective embedding of dynamical systems: uniform mean-field equations (Physica D 450, 133747, 2023)
  • Forest expansion of two-body partition functions for sparse interaction graphs (Physica A 609, 128345, 2023)
  • Some exactly solvable and tunable frustrated spin models (Physica A 594, 127007, 2022)
  • The Bellman equation and optimal reversible flipping strategies for kinetic Ising models (Physica A 649, 129956, 2024)
  • Structural health monitoring in the context of non-equilibrium phase transitions (Data Science in Engineering vol. 10, SEM 2023)
  • Quantum quenches and thermalization on scale-free graphs (J. Theor. Appl. Phys. 8, 82, 2014)

Spectral, Combinatorial and Operator Methods

Complex Systems, Networks and Economic Structure

Large economic and ecological systems are networks with heterogeneous, partially observed structure, and the quantities of interest — influence, systemic risk, position in a production chain — are functionals of that structure. Using the cavity method and random-matrix techniques I derive the full distributions of such quantities over graph ensembles, rather than single-network point estimates, and identify when local information suffices to reconstruct aggregate behaviour.

Quantum Gravity, Cosmology and Fundamental Physics

My earlier work, including my doctoral thesis, concerned pre-geometric models of quantum gravity: theories in which space is not assumed but emerges from the dynamics of a graph or a spin system, with the metric, locality and dispersion relations appearing as low-temperature or coarse-grained properties. Related work covers group field theory, black-hole effective actions and geometric phases in cosmology. The recurring theme — that geometry and computation may both be emergent descriptions of an underlying combinatorial dynamics — continues to inform the projects above.

  • Disordered locality and Lorentz dispersion relations: an explicit model of quantum foam (Phys. Rev. D 86, 024019, 2012)

Teaching, Outreach and Interactive Simulations
