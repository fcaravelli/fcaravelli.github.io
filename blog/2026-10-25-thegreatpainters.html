  • 1. David Heinemeier Hansson and the painters of the past

    • A few weeks ago, David Heinemeier Hansson gave the opening keynote at the RailsConf. He is a very well known programmer, the inventor of Ruby on Rails, and in my opinion, a very good speaker and motivator - and a race car driver! This was an awaited keynote, as currently any programmer is going through a very difficult time, with the rise of AI and the fear of being replaced by it. Now, I have using computers since I was 8 years old. I vividly remember my dad bringing home a 386 computer, I think a Deskpro. I don't know exactly why he used it, but it was a great machine for its time, and he was using the early softwares for electrical engineering.

    For me it was a quite mysterious object. Windows did not exist yet, it would have arrived in the mid 90s, and the computer was running MS-DOS. Fast forward a few years, and I was using computers for programming, initially in Visual Basic. Let me say that I have never been a great programmer, my interest shifted to Physics when I was around 15. Around 2000, at the turn of the century, me and my friend Pietro, who lived roughly 200 meters from me, were told that we were not supposed to use the telephone to communicate, as this was costly. There was no "data plan yet", and you paid per minute.

    We were probably 13 or 14 years old. He was an electronics enthusiast, and he had a computer at home. I was more interested in programming at the time, and I told him: let's build a device to communicate with each other. He custom built a device with an antenna, I build a software to communicate with it and decode the signal. To my surprise, the device worked. We never manage to reach the range we needed to talk to each other, but we were able to communicate with a few meters of distance. This was my first experience with "making things happen". There is a particularly rewarding feeling when you build something that works. I went on to study Physics, while he went on to study electronics. I have always been a computer enthusiast, although this was on pause between 2004 and 2016 essentially, a hiatus due to my interest in (theoretical) Physics. Around 2016, I started to get interested in memristors (for reasons I will get into in a future post), and I started to get back into programming, and electronics. to make theoretical studies and predictions about the behavior of many of these components. Every year I say to myself: this is the last year I work on memristive devices, and every year I discover something new and exciting. It is a nice intersection between physics, complex systems, computing and electronics, and programming these systems has nothing to do with "programming" per se.

    Going back to to the keynote, DHH made a very interesting parallel between the great painters at the time of introduction of photography (go to his talk here to see it), and the current situation of programmers with the rise of AI. Till roughly 1890, painters were the only way to capture a moment in time. The upper bourgeoisie and the royal families would commission painters to paint their portraits, and the painters would be paid handsomely for their work, spending months. It must have been horrific to be a painter at the time, as you would have to paint the same person over and over again. Between the early 1900 and 1930, photography became cheaper and cheaper, a perfect example of the result of how technological change affect society. While still expensive, 1$ in 1900 is worth roughly 40$ today (check here), it was faster and cheaper than hiring a a painter.

    Then photography was invented, and it was a cheaper way to capture a moment in time. People till paint today. Indeed, the great painters either switched to photography, or indeed cubism and the abstract art movement were, in a way, a reaction to the advent of photography; painters were trying to find a new way to express themselves without relying on strict realism. And the advent of computers into chess and then go did not stop people from playing these games. However, these are, except for a few people, a hobby. It is very hard to make money with either of them unless you are a few of the best players in the world. The same was not true for programming and painters, however. These were in fact professions.
